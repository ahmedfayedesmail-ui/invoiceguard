# STAGE-1-REMAINING-RISKS (Nov 27 2026, as of 7380abf plus the lab branch)
Counts are local. This list is for Ahmed's decisions. Nothing here was changed beyond the commits listed in NIGHT-SHIFT-EXECUTION-REPORT.md.

1. Real-data risk (highest). Every rule was checked on synthetic and fixture data only. No real VIDR was seen. The rounding tolerance (1 cent, 2 cents for quantity above 1) is an unproven hypothesis, and Amazon states that from 1 June 2026 VCS calculates tax per shipment and tax rate instead of per unit (https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/changelog/vat-calculation-service-updates, 2026-03-25). The first real post-June VIDR must be used to confirm or replace the tolerance and the per-line arithmetic assumption. Until then INFO notes keep every difference visible and nothing is hidden.
2. Rule R11 gap (decision needed): a negative non-promo amount in a shipment with a positive total is issued. Todo test in test/financial-adversarial.test.js.
3. Far-future shipment dates are accepted (no upper bound on date). Proposal: hold dates later than the evaluation time plus a stated margin. Needs a decision, since time zones make a margin necessary.
4. Refund and repair path (src/repair.js, credit notes from report amounts) is outside Stage 1. It has a human approval and an exact-cent cross-check, but no integrity gate on the refund rows themselves (no field-conflict check across the rows of a refund).
5. Ledger growth: one history row (RECHECKED_SAME) per open finding per evaluation. Measured: 714 open findings re-evaluated 4 more times added 2856 history rows (lab/perf/perf-night.mjs on the lab branch, local). With a sync every few minutes and persistent holds this grows without bound. The one-row policy is in the reviewed design (section 5), so I did not change it. Proposal: count consecutive RECHECKED_SAME in the finding (seen_count already exists) and write history only on state or fingerprint change, after Ahmed agrees.
6. Two processes on one SQLite file rely on BEGIN IMMEDIATE and unique indexes. Tested with two connections in one process, not two machines. Production runs one process.
7. Database governance is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof: anyone with write access to the file can drop a trigger. The audit chain and the append-only history make changes visible.
8. A flaky browser test: test/vault-ui.test.js "delete one import ... restore" timed out once in CI (run 46068796567, 30000 ms wait, passed on the same run's rerun and in all other runs). Cause not proven. Evidence so far: 4 of 4 local repeats passed; under three busy CPU loops 5 runs passed and the 6th did not finish within my 40 s limit (its result was not read), which fits a timing sensitivity but does not prove it. It is a Phase A browser test and the changed code does not touch it. It needs repeated runs under load to find the cause.
