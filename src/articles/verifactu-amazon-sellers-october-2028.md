
# Verifactu and Amazon sellers: what to do now that the deadline moved to October 2028

If you sell on Amazon from Spain, you have probably heard "Verifactu" from three different people and understood none of them the same way. The date just changed again. Here is what is confirmed, what is not, and a plan that does not depend on guessing.

*General information only, not tax or legal advice. Ask your gestor how this applies to you.*

## What changed

On 5 October 2026 the Ministry of Finance announced it planned to postpone the pending obligations of the invoicing systems regulation (Real Decreto 1007/2023) until October 2028. For weeks that was only an announcement. The legal dates were still 1 January 2027 for companies and 1 July 2027 for self-employed workers.

That is no longer the case. Real Decreto-ley 43/2026, dated 24 November, was published in the BOE on 25 November 2026. The Spanish tax agency states that invoicing systems must be adapted before **6 October 2028**.

The new date lines up with the end of the deadline for mandatory electronic invoicing between businesses for companies and professionals under 8 million euros of annual turnover.

Sources: Agencia Tributaria news item of 25 November 2026 (sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es) and BOE-A-2026-24701.

## What did not change

- The technical requirements stay substantially the same: integrity, retention, accessibility, legibility, traceability and inalterability of invoicing records.
- Mandatory B2B e-invoicing (the Crea y Crece law) is a separate obligation with its own calendar.
- Your Amazon obligations did not move at all.

## Does Verifactu apply to my Amazon invoices?

This is the question that matters, and the honest answer is: it depends on how you issue them. Verifactu concerns the software or systems that Spanish businesses use to issue invoices. If you issue your Amazon invoices with a system, that system is in scope of the regulation like any other. If Amazon generates them for you through its VAT Calculation Service, the question is different. Your gestor should look at your real setup rather than a general rule.

Do not rely on a forum answer for this, including this one.

## What did not wait: Amazon's invoice deadline

Amazon's Business invoicing policy requires a valid VAT invoice for each Amazon Business order within one business day after dispatch is confirmed, and credit notes within one business day of return confirmation. That rule applies today regardless of any Spanish deadline. Sellers who cannot do it reliably by hand are the ones who feel the pain first.

## A practical plan

**Now**
1. Write down how each kind of invoice is produced today: Amazon-generated, your own software, spreadsheet, or a third-party tool.
2. Ask your gestor two direct questions: which of my invoices fall under the invoicing systems regulation, and what does my software provider say about adapting?
3. Check that your Amazon B2B invoices are on time and in sequence. This costs nothing and protects your account.

**In 2027**
4. If you use a spreadsheet or Word template for any invoice, plan the move to a proper system. You have time, so compare two or three options and check whether adaptation is included in the price.
5. Ask your current software vendor for their written adaptation date.

**Before October 2028**
6. Confirm with your gestor that the systems you use are adapted. Keep the evidence.

## Short answers

**Is Verifactu cancelled?** No. The adaptation deadline moved to before 6 October 2028.

**Do I need to do anything this year?** Probably not for Verifactu itself. Check your Amazon invoice routine, which has a one-business-day deadline already.

**Who decides if I am in scope?** Your gestor, based on how you actually issue invoices.
